Participant-level agreement for QRS and QT duration. Each point represents one participant and each panel one output head. The horizontal coordinate is the participant’s mean of (prediction + reference)/2. The vertical coordinate is the participant’s mean signed error (prediction − reference). Each model head first averaged the duration estimates from its three fixed RDB-trained fits for each event. We then averaged equally over a participant’s eligible events. The solid line shows the unweighted mean of the participant-specific errors. The grey dashed line marks zero error. A–B, LUDB global QRS intervals with complete references in all 12 leads and successful matching by all six fits (1,742 of 1,786 eligible beats; 196 of 197 eligible participants; 1–19 common beats per contributing participant). Global reference durations were assembled from the native lead-specific marks. C–D, Berlin QT intervals on the selected expert-labelled beat of each recording, measured by all six fits (547 of 548 eligible recordings; all 289 eligible participants; 1–7 common recordings per participant). The spread describes participant averages; limits of agreement for individual beats and clinical equivalence were not estimated. Axis scales differ by row. Reference-denominated coverage, including missed measurements, is reported separately.

Inputs: the stored event-level outputs from the Multilead_LUDB_validation.zip and Berlin_PTB_QT_external.zip study archives. Verify source digests against input_hashes.json. The CSV contains 970 data rows: 196 + 289 participants for each of two heads. For a CSV-only replay, run python figure.py --from-points. To rebuild all data from the extracted analysis packages, run python figure.py --qrs PATH_TO_LUDB_PARENT --qt PATH_TO_BERLIN_PARENT. In the CSV, pair_mean_ms and error_ms are the exact horizontal and vertical coordinates. Equal participant means are descriptive and use a different weighting from the pooled-event analyses.
