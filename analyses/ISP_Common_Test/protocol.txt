Fixed third-cohort test on ISP lead II, 25 November 2026

Record this analysis specification before applying any model to ISP under this protocol. This is a retrospective addition; previous analyses in the broader project included ISP. The ten fixed head/seed configurations to be scored here were trained and selected using RDB or LUDB. No fitting, threshold selection or checkpoint selection is performed on ISP.

Study question. On the same external recordings, does replacing a GELU output head by a capacity-matched KAN head change the mean six-boundary F1 for each of two source training protocols? Primary readout: KAN minus GELU at exact ±150 ms, separately for RDB training (three fixed seeds; one model per seed/head) and LUDB training (two fixed seeds; a five-fold probability ensemble per seed/head). Secondary: ±40 ms and the individual boundaries and seeds. Do not test or attribute a between-training-source effect: sample size, training protocol and ensembling differ.

Population. Use all usable ISP v2 records (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.14679837, source ZIP MD5 a070d5f7d5ff88ce823dbedc7412ee28). Its released partitions are both evaluation data here. Identify each record by partition and original file name. Use the lead explicitly named II in the WFDB header; use the recording's declared sampling frequency and the physical waveform. Exclude unreadable or unannotated records, non-finite lead II inputs and exact repeated raw waveform payloads (keep first in train-then-test numeric order). Record reasons. Patient IDs beyond source record identifiers are unavailable. One global annotation set is evaluated once per recording; it need not coincide with the visually optimal boundary on lead II. The 475 source files are not 475 × 12 independent observations.

Annotation rules. Decode source tuples as P=0, QRS=1, T=2, native sample coordinates. Skip unknown classes, reversed or zero-length segments. Keep individual observed boundaries: exclude a start or end outside the sampled range, or exactly at recording start/end, as censored; the valid opposite endpoint remains eligible. Remove duplicate timing marks within the same class and boundary type. Preserve native timing for references, use 4-ms increments for outputs. Use the span of the observed annotations (minimum to maximum retained reference landmark). Permit matches within tolerance near its edges; count remaining predictions as false positives only within the inclusive scored span. Record annotation anomalies, scored span, missing wave classes and wide successive QRS-onset gaps (>2 seconds) descriptively. Absence of a marked wave can reflect both physiology and incomplete annotation; no signal-based relabeling.

Fixed inference. Polyphase resample to 250 Hz, zero-phase 0.5–40 Hz second-order Butterworth filtering, center by median and scale by IQR/1.349 with the original fallback. Use the original 2,048-sample Hann-tapered overlapping windows, probabilities averaged over five folds when applicable, argmax and fixed post-processing. Merge gaps <5 samples; minimum inclusive P, QRS, T durations are 10, 10 and 15 samples respectively. Omit a predicted boundary at the first/last sample; retain its opposite observable boundary. Both training sources use identical signal processing, scoring and cohort.

Statistics. Match each reference/detection at most once, increasing absolute distance with stable tie handling. Pool TP/FN/FP across recordings, compute F1 for each of six boundary types, take their unweighted mean per seed and then the mean over that source's fixed seeds. Use 10,000 record-level paired bootstrap draws (NumPy seed 20261125, shared across all fits, both heads, both tolerances and all six boundaries). Extract 2.5th/97.5th percentiles of each within-source difference. No confidence claim about retraining variability, general clinical equivalence or independent confirmation of a prespecified primary trial. Report all results.
