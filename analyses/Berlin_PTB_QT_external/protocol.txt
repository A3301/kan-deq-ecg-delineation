External transfer to the Berlin PTB Diagnostic ECG Database. Dated 25 November 2026 before inference on any PTB signal.

Design. Test all six previously frozen networks trained on RDB lead II, three fitted seeds per KAN/GELU head, on the Berlin PTB cohort. No model or operating point is selected on the PTB dataset. Use the 1.0.0 PhysioNet release and the published final Med Q onset and Med T end, in ms from start, in Christov et al. (2006) Additional file 1, Table 4. These are the authors' final consensus coordinates, not necessarily a freshly computed median of the five displayed columns. A record with a missing, nonpositive, reversed, or out-of-window consensus pair is ineligible. The paper describes one visually selected representative beat per recording, annotated in lead II with cross-lead recourse for difficult T ends. No other beats are labelled negatives. Release metadata group recordings by PTB participant directory. Keep all eligible repeat recordings and cluster resampling by this ID. PTB Berlin, RDB Chapman-derived China, LUDB Russia and ISP are independently documented sources; no cross-database universal participant identifiers are available.

Signal. Infer once on the first fixed ten seconds of native lead II from every record independently of the annotated times. Original PTB files are interleaved integer 16 at 1 kHz; use published header channel order, ADC baseline and gain. Verify SHA256 of every complete released signal and header before reading. Resample to 250 Hz with scipy resample_poly then the source Butterworth 0.5-40 Hz second-order zero-phase bandpass, median and IQR/1.349 normalisation (SD fallback). Use original two 2048-sample overlapping prediction windows, probability taper, class argmax, merge-gap and class minimum lengths. We examine one selected beat/case rather than exhaustive full-record beat detection.

Pairing. After predicting independently, identify the QRS run whose onset is closest to the published QRS onset within 150 ms (tie: earlier). This query identifies the labelled beat, but the predicted onset and offset provide the QT duration. Use the first retained T wave starting strictly after that predicted QRS ends and ending before the subsequent predicted QRS starts. Without a following QRS use the recording end. Abstain if either a QRS or an attached T is unavailable or if the T extends to the right edge. No duration acceptance limit, no proximity criterion relative to the published T end for attachment. Reference QT is the published T end minus published Q onset, without heart-rate correction.

Primary. For each head/seed, compute over ALL eligible reference recordings the percentage with a returned QT whose absolute duration error is at most 40 ms. Count unreturned QT as unsuccessful. Average this percentage across the three fixed seeds, and report KAN minus GELU (percentage points). This 40-ms engineering tolerance is not a clinical acceptability margin. Additional estimates: return coverage, success at 20 and 60 ms; success when both landmark errors are <=40 or <=150 ms, always including failures. Conditional paired QT duration MAE and signed error using the exact same beats returned by BOTH heads in ALL THREE seeds; scope and common-set coverage always accompany these results. Report per-landmark absolute errors as secondary and report each model's all-returned MAE to show conditioning. Primary computation pools records, patient-cluster percentile CIs use the same 10,000 draws across models and seeds; secondary equal-patient weighting averages each subject's per-record success first. No cross-cohort aggregation or claim for six-boundary accuracy, QTc or unassisted clinical QT measurement.

Reader context. Describe the within-case range of the five reader QT estimates in the released final adjudicated table; reader measurements are correlated by adjudication and are not independent estimates of a clinical noise floor.
