PTB-XL manual-mask release: accession and time-coordinate audit
25 November 2026

Outcome. The manual-mask release and its accession list are publicly accessible and verified. We could not establish an unambiguous mapping from its 1,200-element annotation grid to the original 10-second ECG under the released accession order. The reference was excluded from model performance estimation. No study models were loaded or scored. The 957 records from 899 verification patients were not used in any time-mapping calculation.

Input and independence. The immutable Hugging Face revision c9d0577dc2bb82a6738e6d5dce1451aae4b09904 contains two arrays of 2,604 × 1,200 and 10,728 × 1,200 float64 values. We verified the Git LFS SHA256 for each payload and the Git blob hash for the record lists, database CSV and card. The lists identify 217 AF and 894 other records, 1,111 unique ECGs from 1,049 patients. The accompanying patient table is byte-identical to original PTB-XL 1.0.3. The twelve contiguous label rows per accession were byte-for-byte equal in all 1,111 records. This release can at most provide a synchronous reference. Public documentation describes physician/physiologist input but does not specify which state ID is P, QRS or T, the mask frequency, crop origin, or an explicit ordered row-to-signal contract. These gaps preclude calling the twelve copies independent expert lead markings. The patients’ correspondence to the older PTB Diagnostic archive was not assessed.

Coordinate audit. We saved the analysis rules before examining waveform or fiducial alignment and assigned entire patients by a fixed ID hash to calibration (150 patients, 154 ECGs) or verification (899 patients, 957 ECGs). For the calibration records we retrieved all 154 original 500-Hz waveform/header pairs and independent ECGDeli global fiducial landmarks. All 1,111 global landmark files, 308 waveform/header files, and the official PTB-XL metadata table verified against publisher SHA256. We evaluated ten physiologically plausible candidate frequencies (120–500 Hz), feasible global crop starts in 10-ms increments plus exact start/centre/end candidates, and all six nonbackground state permutations. This produced 25,578 candidate mappings. For each candidate, we assessed the proportion of complete mask intervals containing the corresponding independent P, R or T peak, giving each available type and record equal weight. These automated fiducials served only to verify time coordinates.

Peak containment peaked at 37.48% on the calibration group (120 Hz, origin zero, states 1/2/3 interpreted as QRS/P/T). An alternative using 125 Hz and a different ordering reached 36.97%. At 200 and 250 Hz, the best combinations reached 24.89% and 20.33%. These percentages measure alignment of two sources and are not landmark-detection F1 or accuracy of either study head. The low best containment offers no defensible clock, codebook or label ordering for measuring six-boundary F1 at tolerances of 40 or 150 ms. Independent quality checks reproduced the intended mapping in a synthetic positive control. In all 154 records the median native lead-II slope near ECGDeli R peaks exceeded that in same-length windows 160 ms earlier (1,808 R peaks checked). Even origin-independent inter-event spacing of the three nonzero classes correlated weakly with the official R–R interval (Spearman 0.04–0.12, descriptive).

What the public inputs can establish. Source access, accession/patient counts, 12-fold label replication, the diagnostic-subgroup arrangement, and the inability to validate a single global time transform under the checked correspondence. We cannot identify whether row ordering, frequency, crop origin, another transformation or a combination explains the discrepancy. We made no record-specific rearrangement or time-warping attempt. Future evaluation would require the depositor's ordered ID-to-mask manifest, class dictionary, and executable or exact conversion of mask index to original recording time, including any record-specific crop start. These requests have not been sent.

Suggested manuscript/supplement line. “We assessed a public manual PTB-XL mask release covering 1,111 linked ECGs. Its 1,200-sample annotation grid could not be reliably aligned with the native waveforms in a model-independent, patient-separated calibration audit. We therefore retained this release in the source inventory and excluded it from performance estimation.”
